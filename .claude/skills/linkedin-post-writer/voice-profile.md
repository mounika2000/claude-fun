## My tone of voice
- Overall vibe: warm, reflective, human-first, growth-minded, optimistic, lightly playful.
  Smart but never lecturing; vulnerable but not performative.
- Sentence style: short, clean lines with generous white space. A strong one-line takeaway
  near the end that's eminently quotable. Occasional playful touches — emojis, a pop-culture
  or anime reference (e.g. Frieren), the odd strikethrough wordplay (manager → leader).
- Signature structure I lean on:
  1. A bold, curiosity- or tension-driven hook (NOT "I'm excited to share").
  2. A concrete personal moment or scene.
  3. Numbered or arrow (→) lessons/realizations, often 3-5, sometimes with bold mini-labels
     ("The Proximity Effect", "The Conductor vs. the Builder").
  4. One punchy thesis line.
  5. An engagement CTA ("Tag a leader 👇", "send it my way", "share it with your team").
- Emojis: yes, used sparingly and purposefully (🌍 👇 🤓 ⬇️ →).
- Things I'd NEVER say: corporate buzzword soup, "humbled to announce", "game-changer",
  "delve", "synergy", hype without substance. No bragging — credibility shows through stories.

## My recurring themes / takes (the throughline)
The human side of tech wins. Specific beliefs I come back to:
- People and proximity beat docs and process — "Human proximity is still the ultimate catalyst."
- Compassion is the difference between a manager and a leader.
- As AI handles execution, our value shifts from builder to conductor: taste, judgment, and
  connecting unrelated dots matter more than raw syntax.
- Play is a serious form of work — breakthroughs come from low-stakes experimentation.
- Agency: you're the main character in your own career; plans are a starting point, not a script.
- Relationships and mentorship compound; the industry is smaller than it looks.
- Sharing freely (open-source the workshop, "[Steal this]") builds community and trust.
